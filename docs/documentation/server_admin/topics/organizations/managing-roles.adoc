[[_managing_roles_]]
=  Managing roles
[role="_abstract"]

Organization roles let you define coarse-grained permissions that are scoped to a single organization. Use them to model responsibilities such as `org-admin` or `billing-manager` without affecting the rest of the realm.

Unlike realm roles that are shared across your entire realm, organization roles belong exclusively to one organization. This means `orgA` and `orgB` can each have an `org-admin` role without any conflict—each is a completely separate role with its own unique identifier.

== Creating roles

Navigate to your organization and click the *Roles* tab.

.Procedure

. Click *Create role*.
. Provide a *Name* for the role.
. Optionally, provide a *Description*.
. Click *Create*.

Your role is ready. You can now assign it to members, add attributes, or associate other roles with it.

== Understanding the default role

Every organization automatically has a default role, named `default-roles-<alias>`, that is created when the organization is created and shown with a *Default* badge.

All organization members are automatically granted this role—there's no need to assign it manually. Use it to define permissions that should apply to every member of the organization.

The default role cannot be deleted, and it doesn't expose the *Attributes* or *Users in role* tabs, since it isn't meant to be managed like a regular role.

== Assigning roles to members

Members can be granted an organization role directly, without going through a group.

.Procedure

. Select the role.
. Click the *Users in role* tab.
. Click *Add user*.
. Select one or more organization members.
. Click *Add*.

To remove a member from the role, select the member in the *Users in role* tab and click *Remove*.

TIP: You can also assign organization roles to a group of members at once. See <<_managing_groups_,Managing groups>> for details.

=== Assigning roles automatically with identity providers

If your organization is linked to an identity provider, you can grant an organization role automatically to members who authenticate through that identity provider by using the *Hardcoded Organization Role* identity provider mapper.

.Procedure

. Click *Identity providers* in the menu and select the identity provider linked to the organization.
. Click the *Mappers* tab.
. Click *Add mapper*.
. Provide a *Name* for the mapper.
. Select a *Sync mode override* according to when the role should be granted (for example, on first login only or on every login).
. Set *Mapper type* to *Hardcoded Organization Role*.
. Select the *Organization Role* to grant.
. Click *Save*.

When a user authenticates through the identity provider, the selected role is granted automatically. The identity provider must be linked to an enabled organization, and the role must belong to that organization.

== Associating roles

Organization roles support composites, just like realm and client roles. An organization role can be associated with:

* Other roles from the same organization
* Realm roles
* Client roles

.Procedure

. Select the role.
. Click the *Associated roles* tab.
. Click *Assign role*.
. Select one or more organization, realm, or client roles.
. Click *Assign*.

When a member is granted a role, they automatically inherit all of its associated roles. Composite roles are expanded automatically wherever roles are resolved.

== Managing role attributes

Roles can carry attributes—key/value metadata stored at the role level.

.Procedure

. Select a role.
. Click the *Attributes* tab.
. Add attributes with keys and values.
. Click *Save*.

NOTE: The default role does not support attributes.

== Mapping roles to tokens

Organization role membership can appear in tokens for both OIDC and SAML protocols. The setup differs between protocols.

IMPORTANT: The *Organization Role Membership* mapper alone is NOT sufficient. It only works when combined with the *Organization Membership* mapper in the same scope.

=== OIDC: Organization roles in tokens

==== Option 1: Add mapper to built-in organization scope (Recommended)

The built-in `organization` scope already has the Organization Membership mapper. Simply add the role mapper to it.

.Procedure

. Click *Client scopes* in the menu.
. Select the *organization* scope.
. Click the *Mappers* tab.
. Click *Add mapper* → *By configuration*.
. Select *Organization Role Membership*.
. Configure the mapper:

Add to ID token::
Include roles in ID tokens.

Add to access token::
Include roles in access tokens.

Add to userinfo::
Include roles in the UserInfo endpoint response.

Add to introspection::
Include roles in the Introspection endpoint response.

. Click *Save*.

==== Option 2: Create custom scope with both mappers

If you need a dedicated scope for organization data, create one with BOTH required mappers.

.Procedure

. Click *Client scopes* in the menu.
. Click *Create client scope*.
. Provide a *Name* (for example, `my-org-scope`).
. Set *Protocol* to *OpenID Connect*.
. Click *Save*.
. Click the *Mappers* tab.
. Add BOTH mappers:
.. Click *Configure a new mapper* → *Organization Membership*
.. Configure and save
.. Click *Add mapper* → *By configuration* → *Organization Role Membership*
.. Configure and save
. Go to your client.
. Click *Client scopes* tab.
. Click *Add client scope*.
. Select your custom scope.
. Click *Add*.
. Choose *Default* or *Optional*.

==== Token structure

When a member directly assigned the `org-admin` organization role authenticates and requests the organization scope, the token includes:

[source,json]
----
{
  "organization": {
    "orgA": {
      "id": "f8d3c4e1-...",
      "roles": [ "org-admin" ]
    }
  }
}
----

If the `org-admin` role has associated realm or client roles, they are included as well:

[source,json]
----
{
  "organization": {
    "orgA": {
      "id": "f8d3c4e1-...",
      "roles": [ "org-admin" ],
      "realm_access": {
        "roles": [ "viewer" ]
      },
      "resource_access": {
        "my-client": {
          "roles": [ "client-editor" ]
        }
      }
    }
  }
}
----

Composite roles are automatically expanded—if an organization role has an associated composite role, all its child roles are included.

NOTE: Organization roles are never added to the top-level `realm_access` or `resource_access` claims. They only appear under the `organization` claim. However, realm and client roles associated with an organization role are added to the standard `realm_access`/`resource_access` claims whenever the member effectively holds them (for example, by having the organization role granted directly or through a group)—no additional mapper configuration is required for those.

=== SAML: Organization roles in assertions

==== Option 1: Add mapper to built-in saml_organization scope (Recommended)

The built-in `saml_organization` scope already has the Organization Membership mapper and is added as a default scope to all SAML clients. Simply add the role mapper to it.

.Procedure

. Click *Client scopes* in the menu.
. Select the *saml_organization* scope.
. Click the *Mappers* tab.
. Click *Add mapper* → *By configuration*.
. Select *Organization Role Membership*.
. Click *Save*.

==== Option 2: Create custom SAML scope with both mappers

If you need a dedicated scope, create one with both the membership and role mappers.

.Procedure

. Click *Client scopes* in the menu.
. Click *Create client scope*.
. Provide a *Name* (for example, `my-saml-org-scope`).
. Set *Protocol* to *SAML*.
. Click *Save*.
. Click the *Mappers* tab.
. Add BOTH mappers:
.. Click *Configure a new mapper* → *Organization Membership*
.. Click *Save*
.. Click *Add mapper* → *By configuration* → *Organization Role Membership*
.. Click *Save*
. Go to your SAML client.
. Click *Client scopes* tab.
. Click *Add client scope*.
. Select your custom scope.
. Click *Add*.
. Choose *Default* or *Optional*.

==== SAML assertion structure

When a member directly assigned the `org-admin` organization role authenticates, the assertion includes attributes for each organization they belong to:

[source,xml]
----
<Attribute Name="organization.orgA.roles">
  <AttributeValue>org-admin</AttributeValue>
</Attribute>
<Attribute Name="organization.orgA.realm_access.roles">
  <AttributeValue>viewer</AttributeValue>
</Attribute>
<Attribute Name="organization.orgA.resource_access.my-client.roles">
  <AttributeValue>client-editor</AttributeValue>
</Attribute>
----

Each organization gets its own attributes named `+organization.{alias}.roles+`, `+organization.{alias}.realm_access.roles+`, and `+organization.{alias}.resource_access.{clientId}.roles+`, where `alias` is the organization alias.

== Important distinctions

=== Organization roles vs realm roles

[cols="1,2", options="header"]
|===
|Feature
|Organization roles

|Scope
|Belong to a single organization

|Isolation
|Fully isolated—the same role name can exist across organizations

|Composite roles
|Can compose other organization roles, as well as realm and client roles

|Authorization policies
|Cannot be used in authorization policies—use realm or client roles instead

|Token mapping
|Appear in tokens only when using organization context, and only under the `organization` claim
|===

=== Authorization policies restriction

Organization roles *cannot* be used in Keycloak authorization policies. Only realm and client roles are supported there.

If you try to select an organization role when configuring a role-based policy or a client scope mapping, you'll receive an error. This is intentional—authorization policies and scope mappings operate at the realm level, not the organization level.

NOTE: Managing organization roles requires the `manage-organizations` realm admin role (or equivalent Fine-Grained Admin Permission on the organization). Assigning a role to a member additionally requires the `map-role` permission on the role and the `manage-users` permission on the target user. Listing or viewing organization roles requires `view-organizations` (or the equivalent Fine-Grained Admin Permission).

== Deleting roles

.Procedure

. Select the role.
. Click the *Delete role* action.
. Confirm the deletion.

Members who were assigned the role lose the associated permissions. The default role cannot be deleted.
